    Where does everyone stand?

    Loktantra Atlas maps India's political parties and freedom-era thinkers across the debates like the state vs the market, faith vs the secular or the Centre vs the states, that continue to shape the republic. But no party is a single idea. Each is a bundle of positions. You may favour one party's free-market economics yet reject its cultural stance, or admire another's social vision while doubting its regulated economy. That tension isn't a flaw in you; it's the whole point. This atlas helps you think issue by issue and draw your own map

    01 · The Framework

    Why two axes, not one

    Indian politics is usually flattened onto a single line - left at one end, right at the other. But one line cannot hold two very different questions at once: who should run the economy, and how much should faith and tradition shape public life. Two parties can stand together on markets and yet worlds apart on culture or the reverse. So instead of one line, we use two crossing axes, turning a flat spectrum into a map. Every debate card and every dot below is a point where these two questions meet.

    Horizontal · X axis

    Left ↔ Right: the economy

    Who drives growth and welfare? One pole trusts the state - planning, subsidies, public ownership, redistribution. The other trusts the market - competition, private enterprise, open trade. Where a party sits here says who it believes should steer the economy.

    Vertical · Y axis

    Up ↔ Down: culture & faith

    How much should religion, heritage and tradition guide the state? One pole leans toward public faith and majoritarian culture; the other toward secularism and social reform, keeping the state an equal distance from every religion. This axis captures what the economic line misses.

    Putting it together

    How to read the map

    The six debates that follow are the raw arguments. We distil each party's stance across them into two coordinates, placing it in one of four quadrants. So a dot is not a label — it's a bundle of positions. Compare dots, not names, and the picture stops being about teams and starts being about ideas.

    03 · The Map

    The Indian political compass

    Left–right asks who runs the economy. Up–down asks how much tradition and faith should shape public life. Hover any point for a preview; click to inspect. Toggle layers to compare today's parties with the freedom movement's thinkers.

    Inspect a point

    Click any circle (party) or diamond (freedom-era figure) on the compass to see its position explained.

    Methodology: positions are simplified editorial estimates on −10…+10 axes, intended for civic education, not definitive judgement. Living traditions resist any map.
