    01 · The Framework

    Why two axes, not one

    Indian politics is usually flattened onto a single line - left at one end, right at the other. But one line cannot hold two very different questions at once: who should run the economy, and how much should faith and tradition shape public life. Two parties can stand together on markets and yet worlds apart on culture or the reverse. So instead of one line, we use two crossing axes, turning a flat spectrum into a map. Every debate card and every dot below is a point where these two questions meet. The 'Section 04 - The Real Issues' discusses few of many real moot points that should concern every citizen of the republic. Hence this is essentially A map of ideas, not idols - for every party's bhakts, yours included.

    Horizontal · X axis

    Left ↔ Right: the economy

    Who drives growth and welfare? One pole trusts the state - planning, subsidies, public ownership, redistribution. The other trusts the market - competition, private enterprise, open trade. Where a party sits here says who it believes should steer the economy.

    Vertical · Y axis

    Up ↔ Down: culture & faith

    How much should religion, heritage and tradition guide the state? One pole leans toward public faith and majoritarian culture; the other toward secularism and social reform, keeping the state an equal distance from every religion. This axis captures what the economic line misses.

    Putting it together

    How to read the map

    The six debates that follow are the raw arguments. We distil each party's stance across them into two coordinates, placing it in one of four quadrants. So a dot is not a label, it's a bundle of positions. Compare dots, not names, and the picture stops being about teams and starts being about ideas.

    04 · The Real Issues

    The issues that outlast every government

    These decide how 1.4 billion lives actually go and they've survived every party in power. That's not an excuse for any party; it's the standard we must hold every party to. The question isn't "who caused them?" It's "who's solving them and what will you do about it at the ballot?"

    AI Literacy

    India ranks among the top countries on raw AI activity and jobs, yet far lower on readiness and quality research. A rural student or regional-language learner can't use AI tools because models lack native Indic support and fast connectivity while urban peers race ahead.

    Sources: Stanford HAI, AI Index 2025 · Oxford Insights, Govt AI Readiness Index 2025

    Environment & Water

    54% of India's area faces high-to-extremely-high water stress; 21 major cities risk running out of groundwater. Bengaluru sends tankers through its tech parks while Punjab's borewells run dry - two crises, no connecting plan.

    Sources: WRI, "3 maps explain India's water risks" · PIB / NITI Aayog, CWMI

    Brain Drain

    Nearly 9 lakh Indians renounced citizenship over five years (~1.6–2 lakh a year). Top engineers and doctors migrate because domestic R&D funding and salaries lag global equivalents. We subsidise their education; they build other nations.

    Source: MEA, reply to Parliament

    Education Quality

    Only about half of Class 5 children can read a Class 2 text, and a large share of 10-year-olds live in "learning poverty." Remote schools face teacher shortages and no labs, pushing kids into costly coaching. The diploma says graduate; the skill says otherwise.

    Sources: ASER 2024, Pratham · World Bank, India Learning Poverty Brief 2024

    Public Healthcare

    Households still pay over 40% of health costs out-of-pocket; a tier-3 family sells land or borrows from moneylenders because the local public hospital lacks diagnostics. Medical bills push millions into poverty yearly.

    Sources: PIB, National Health Accounts 2022-23 · World Bank, OOPE indicator

    Criminalisation of Politics

    46% of 18th Lok Sabha MPs declared criminal cases (31% serious). Parties nominate them for muscle and money, because the system rewards "winnability" over integrity.

    Sources: ADR, 18th Lok Sabha analysis · PRS, Profile of the 18th Lok Sabha

    Eroding Trust in Media

    Only ~36% of Indians trust the news, and India sits near the bottom of press-freedom rankings. Prime-time stages shouting matches over outrage while ignoring rural distress, droughts and layoffs. The news tells you who to hate, not how to live.

    Sources: Reuters Institute, India Digital News Report · RSF, World Press Freedom Index (India)

    Petty Corruption & Red Tape

    India ranks 96/180 on the Corruption Perceptions Index and scores poorly on "control of corruption." The real cost is the daily indignity of paying bribes to get a ration card, pension or land record. The system makes you pay for your own rights.

    Sources: Transparency Intl, CPI 2024 · World Bank, Worldwide Governance Indicators

    Figures are approximate, drawn from the cited government releases, UN/World Bank data and peer-reviewed research.

    05 · The Way Out

    Vote on the issues

    No leader solves these by being worshipped, and no party solves them by being blamed. They get solved when citizens make them the question at every election.

    Treat your vote as an investment in your future not a reward for someone's past.

    Ask, of every candidate, of every party: What's your concrete plan for jobs, AI readiness, water, education, healthcare and all the local issues and how will I measure it?

    Whoever is in power works for you. Hold them to it. आत्मदीपो भव।
